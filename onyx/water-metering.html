Water Metering & Water-Loss Investigations

Water loss stays invisible until someone measures for it.

Xflow's metering work covers six measurement technologies, from residential displacement meters through to municipal-scale turbine and electromagnetic systems, plus standalone water-loss investigation.

Metering technology

Six ways to measure flow and consumption.

01

Displacement (PD) Meters

Oscillating piston and nutating disc designs. Water physically displaces the measuring element in direct proportion to flow, giving accurate readings at low-to-moderate residential and small commercial rates.

Size: 5/8″ – 2″ Body: bronze, brass or plastic
02

Velocity Meters

Measures flow velocity through a known internal capacity, then converts that to volume. Covers multi-jet, turbine, propeller and mag-meter variants, each suited to a different flow range, calibrated with an adjustment vane.

Multi-Jet⅝″–2″, accurate at low flow, internal strainer standard
Turbine1-½″–12″+, straight-through path, large commercial & fire protection
Fire MetersUL/FM-rated turbine variant for fire-protection flow
Fire HydrantPortable, aluminium, ~3″ capacity, construction sites
Calibration: adjustment vane
03

Compound Meters

Two measuring elements plus a check valve — a turbine element for high flow, a multi-jet or PD element for low flow — built for sites where demand swings widely between the two.

Best for: variable-demand sites
04

Electromagnetic (Mag) Meters

Uses Faraday's law of induction rather than mechanical parts. Measures flow in either direction and handles raw or waste water without clogging risk.

No strainer required Bi-directional flow capable
05

Ultrasonic Meters

Doppler-effect or transit-time sound waves determine velocity; volume is calculated from a fixed cross-sectional area. High accuracy, low maintenance, long service life.

Flow-through or clamp-on design
06

Prepaid Meters

User purchases water credit in advance via IC/RF card or keypad; credit is consumed as water flows. Common in municipal cost-recovery contexts.

Interface: card, RF or keypad
